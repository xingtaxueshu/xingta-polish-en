To estimate the effect of the reading program, we use a difference-in-differences design which compare the change of reading scores in the treated schools with that in the control schools before and after 2019. The coefficient of the interaction term is 3.42 (p < 0.01), which means that the program increases the reading scores of students by 3.42 points, about 0.15 standard deviations. The result are robust to controlling for the school-level covariates, and the parallel trends assumption is supported by the event-study figure (Figure 2). But we should be cautious on causal interpretation, because the program was not randomly assigned.
