This study bases on Bourdieu's theory of cultural capital as the theoretical framework.[^1] According to Bourdieu, cultural capital exist in three states: the embodied state, the objectified state and the institutionalized state.[^2] The embodied state refers to the long-lasting dispositions of mind and body, the objectified state refers to cultural goods such as books and instruments, the institutionalized state refers to academic qualification. This three-fold distinction is very useful for our study, because it let us to distinguish between what a family possess and what a child actually acquire. In our study, we mainly focus on the embodied cultural capital of the parents, and it's influence on children's reading.

[^1]: Pierre Bourdieu, "The Forms of Capital," in *Handbook of Theory and Research for the Sociology of Education*, ed. John G. Richardson (New York: Greenwood, 1986), 241–258.
[^2]: Bourdieu, "The Forms of Capital," 243.
