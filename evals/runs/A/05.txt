In "The Death of the Author," Barthes claims that "the birth of the reader must be at the cost of the death of the Author."[^1] This sentence is often quoted but seldom analyzed. Some critics take it as a declaration that the author's intention no longer matters; however, Barthes himself did not say this. What he opposes is the idea of the author as the single source of meaning, not the existence of authors. Read this way, the so-called "death" is more a methodological move than a metaphysical statement.

[^1]: Roland Barthes, "The Death of the Author," in *Image—Music—Text*, trans. Stephen Heath (London: Fontana, 1977), 148.
