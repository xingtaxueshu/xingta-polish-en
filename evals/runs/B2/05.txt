In "The Death of the Author," Barthes claims that "the birth of the reader must be at the cost of the death of the Author."[^1] This sentence is often quoted, but seldom analyzed. Some critics take it as a declaration that the author's intention is no longer important; however, Barthes himself did not say that. What he opposes is the idea of the author as a single source of meaning, not the existence of authors. If we read the sentence in this way, then the so-called "death" is more like a methodological move than a metaphysical statement.

[^1]: Roland Barthes, "The Death of the Author," in *Image—Music—Text*, trans. Stephen Heath (London: Fontana, 1977), 148.
